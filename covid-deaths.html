How many people died from covid in the UK, and were they vaccinated?

7 October 2026

Short answer

In England and Wales, from March 2020 to September 2023, covid was mentioned on 208,230 death certificates. In 173,035 of those (about 83%) it was the underlying cause, meaning the person died from covid. The other 35,000 or so died with covid, from something else. Scotland and Northern Ireland are not included, so the UK total is higher.

Of deaths involving covid in England between April 2021 and May 2023, about 85% were among people who had had at least one vaccine dose and about 15% among unvaccinated people. That sounds surprising until you remember that by then nearly everyone in the oldest age groups, who make up most covid deaths, was vaccinated. Comparing like with like by age, people who had a booster had lower covid death rates than unvaccinated people in every month the Office for National Statistics (ONS) measured.

"Died from" versus "died with"

The ONS uses two different measures, both taken from death certificates:

  • Deaths "due to" covid: covid was the underlying cause of death, the condition that started the chain of events leading to death.
  • Deaths "involving" covid: covid was mentioned anywhere on the certificate, including as a contributing factor.

The "due to" figure is the closest official measure of people who died from covid. The gap between the two is roughly the number who died with it.

Caveats

  • Raw shares can mislead. By then most adults, and nearly all over-80s, were vaccinated. Because older people account for most covid deaths, you would expect most deaths to be among vaccinated people even if the vaccine works well. That is why the ONS compared age-standardised death rates instead. From the start of the booster rollout in September 2021 to May 2023, people who had a third dose or booster at least 21 days earlier had lower covid death rates than unvaccinated people in every month.
  • England and Wales only. The death totals above exclude Scotland and Northern Ireland, so the full UK figure is higher. This page does not yet have a sourced UK-wide figure.
  • Early counting was looser. The early headline measure, deaths "within 28 days of a positive test", did include some people who died with covid rather than from it. That is a fair criticism, and it is why the certificate-based "underlying cause" figures used here are the cleaner measure.
  • The vaccination series has ended. The ONS stopped publishing deaths by vaccination status after May 2023 because it no longer receives NHS vaccination records. The December 2023 FOI response was the last one.
